Privacy policy

Last updated 24 November 2026

The short version

  • Relay only reads your email if you connect it, and only in read-only mode. It looks for order confirmations, receipts and return emails, and nothing else.
  • It keeps the details of each purchase (store, items, total, return deadline) and a link back to the email. It does not keep the emails themselves.
  • Your emails are read by Relay's own rules on Relay's own servers. AI help is on for everyone: when those rules can't work out a receipt, Claude, an AI made by Anthropic, reads that one email with your name, addresses, links, phone numbers and card numbers hidden first. AI for picks is on too, and reads only the names of the things you bought. Anthropic can't train its AI on any of it. You can switch either off at any time: tap your picture, then AI help.
  • Relay never sells your data, never shows you ads, and has no tracking or analytics.
  • You can disconnect your email, withdraw your permission or delete your account at any time, from the Account page.

What we collect

Your account

The email address you sign in with. When you sign in, we keep a record that your session is active (stored in scrambled form) so you stay signed in.

What you choose to add

  • Your name and profile photo, if you add them. Only you see them.
  • Receipts you paste in yourself.
  • Which receipts were gifts, and who each was for if you say. Only you see them: they show in your receipts and your Wrapped, and a gift isn't used to work out your own style.
  • Products and shops you ask us to watch for price drops, and your alert settings.
  • Passkeys (Face ID, Touch ID or screen lock sign-in). We keep the public part of the key, a name for the device and when it was last used. Your fingerprint or face never leaves your device.
  • A mobile number, if you add one. We keep it encrypted, show it in the app only as its last three digits (Download my data gives you the whole number), and use it only to text you sign-in codes: never for marketing, and never shown to anyone else.
  • Two-step sign-in, which every account needs: a passkey, codes by text or an authenticator app. We keep your authenticator key encrypted, and your recovery codes and any codes we text you only in scrambled form. With each sign-in we note whether it passed this second step.
  • A card for swaps, if you save one. The card is handled by Stripe; we keep only its brand, last four digits and expiry date.
  • Delivery details, if you add them: your name, a UK delivery address and a phone number for the courier. They're used only to fill in a shop's checkout when you buy something through Relay (see Who sees it). Each time, we note which shop they went to and when, which you can see in Download my data.
  • If you bid in a shop's auction: the most you said you'd pay, when you bid, and anything you won (what you paid, by when, and whether you paid). Nobody else sees who bid or how much your maximum is.
  • If you list something to swap or sell to other members: the listing, your messages with other members, and the first half of your postcode so people can see roughly how far away it is.

Purchases from shops connected to Relay

Some shops connect to Relay through Shopify or Square. When one of them confirms an order of yours, we keep its items and amounts so it appears in your account. For in-store purchases, we may keep a scrambled code made from the card you paid with (never the card number) along with its brand and last four digits, so we can suggest which purchases are yours. When you start a return or swap through Relay, we keep a record of it, including the item, the reason and any tracking number.

Technical information

To stop people repeatedly trying to sign in to accounts, we keep a scrambled form of your IP address with each sign-in request for about a day. When we text a code, we keep a scrambled form of the account, the phone number and the IP address for about two days, so nobody can use Relay to flood a phone with texts; this stays for those two days even if the account is deleted. Our hosting provider keeps short-term logs of requests to Relay's servers for running and fixing the service.

On your device

Relay stores a few things in your browser so it works quickly: your sign-in, a copy of your receipts so they show straight away, and settings like your price alerts. We don't use cookies for advertising or tracking. Signing out removes your sign-in and the copy of your receipts from that device.

Your email

If you connect Gmail or Outlook, you give Relay read-only access. It cannot send, delete, move or change anything in your inbox.

  • What it looks for: order confirmations, receipts and return emails. Other emails are not kept.
  • What it keeps from each one: the store, order number, date, total and items; the email address the shop sent the order to (so a returns page gets the right one); return details such as the deadline, how to return, any return ID or link; one small product photo from the email if there is one; a copy of a barcode or QR code the email gives you to show or scan (to return something in a shop or at a drop-off point, to collect an order, or a ticket), with the shop's own words about it, so it is there when you need it; and a link back to the original email so you can open it yourself.
  • How it reads them: with fixed rules that run on Relay's own servers. Only the emails those rules can't work out go to an AI (AI help, below), unless you switch it off. Your emails are never used to train an AI model.
  • AI help (on unless you switch it off): tap your picture, then AI help. It has been on for everyone since 24 November 2026; before that it was off unless you switched it on. When Relay's rules can't work out a receipt, Claude, an AI made by Anthropic, reads that email to fill in what's missing. It gets the email's text with web links, email addresses, phone numbers, card numbers and codes, postcodes, addresses and your name hidden. Anthropic's terms don't let it train its models on what Relay sends. What the AI fills in is marked in Relay for you to check, never replaces what you corrected yourself, and is deleted when you switch AI help off. While AI help is on, when you list something to sell, Claude also writes a first description from the item's name on your receipt, the shop, and the size and colour if the email gave them (never the price, order number, your address or anything about you); it is marked as written by AI until you change it. With AI help off, the description box starts empty. Relay keeps your choice with your account, so switching it off stays off, even if you disconnect and connect your email again.
  • Read it again (only when you tap it): when the items Relay read on a receipt don't add up to what the email says they came to, the receipt says Relay may have missed some, with Read it again. Tapping it reads that one email again from your inbox with Relay's own rules. If the items still don't add up, and only then, Claude, an AI made by Anthropic, reads the part of that email with the order in it (from just above the first item to the total), with web links, email addresses, phone numbers, card numbers and codes, postcodes, addresses and your name hidden first. It never gets the rest of the email, who sent it or who you are. Relay keeps what it read only if the items it found add up to the email's own total; otherwise nothing it said is kept and the receipt stays as it was. Your tap is your permission for that one email; AI help doesn't need to be on. Anthropic's terms don't let it train its models on what Relay sends. Relay keeps only a count of how many times you used it (at most 20 a day), never the email.
  • AI for picks (on unless you switch it off): tap your picture, then AI help. It has been on for everyone since 24 November 2026. Claude, an AI made by Anthropic, reads the names of the things on your receipts and the shop's name (for example “Align High-Rise Pant” from lululemon) to work out what each one is (for example food and drink, clothing or electronics), so your picks and your Wrapped follow what you really buy. It never gets your emails, address, order numbers or prices, and anything in an item name that looks personal is hidden first. Each name is read once. Anthropic's terms don't let it train its models on what Relay sends. What the AI worked out shapes your picks and how your things are counted (in Wrapped and the receipts list), only when it is sure, and never changes what your receipts say. It is deleted when you switch it off, and Relay stops using it for you. An item name with nothing personal in it (like “Haribo Starmix 140g”) and what the AI said it is are also kept on their own, not linked to you or to any receipt, so the same name doesn't need asking about again. Without AI, Relay's own rules decide, and a thing they can't tell is left as “other”, never guessed.
  • How far back: about the last 16 months. If you ask for a Wrapped of an earlier year, Relay looks through that year as well.
  • Keeping up to date: while your email is connected, Relay looks for new receipts every so often in the background, and straight away when Gmail or Outlook tells it a new email has arrived. That notice says only which inbox has new mail, never anything about the email. Gmail's notices reach Relay through Google Cloud, in Relay's own account there.
  • The key to your inbox: stored encrypted, and used only to look for receipts.
  • Sending an email to Relay: only if you choose to, from a receipt Relay read wrong. You see exactly what will be sent first. Relay hides web links, email addresses, phone numbers, card numbers and codes, postcodes, addresses and your name before it's sent, and you can add a note. A person at Relay reads it to work out why it was read wrong and improve how Relay reads emails like it. They don't see who sent it, and each time one is opened it's recorded. It's kept encrypted, and deleted after 30 days, or sooner if you delete it, remove the receipt, disconnect that inbox, withdraw your permission or delete your account.
  • More than one inbox: you can connect up to five, Gmail or Outlook. Each is read in the same way, and disconnecting one deletes the receipts Relay captured from it.

Information from your Google account

This part says in one place what Relay does with what it gets from Google. It applies as well as everything above.

What Relay gets from Google

  • If you continue with Google: your email address, that Google has checked it is yours, and your name. Relay offers the name for your profile and keeps it only if you save it.
  • If you connect Gmail: read-only access to the emails in that Gmail inbox. Relay searches for order confirmations, receipts, and delivery and return emails, and reads the ones it finds. It can't send, delete, move or change anything.

What Relay uses it for

Only for the things you see in Relay: signing you in, your receipts, return dates and reminders, where a parcel is, codes to show or scan, your Wrapped and your picks. Nothing else.

What Relay keeps, and where

The purchase details listed under Your email, never the emails themselves, in Relay's own database at Cloudflare. The key to your inbox is kept encrypted, and everything travels over encrypted connections.

Who it is passed to

  • Cloudflare, which runs Relay's servers and database.
  • Anthropic, whose AI, Claude, reads the emails Relay's own rules can't work out, with your name, addresses, links, phone numbers and card numbers hidden first, and reads the names of the things you bought to say what kind of thing each is. Relay's permission wording says this before you connect your inbox, and you can switch either off at any time under AI help. Anthropic's terms don't let it train its AI on what Relay sends.
  • A shop, only when you start a return or swap with it yourself: it gets that order's details.
  • Ship24, when you open a purchase whose parcel hasn't arrived: it gets that parcel's tracking number, and nothing else.
  • Resend, only when you asked for a return reminder: it sends you that one email, which names the item and the shop.
  • A person at Relay reads an email of yours only if you choose to send that one email to Relay (see Sending an email to Relay, above).

Nobody else gets it, except where the law requires it or to look into misuse of Relay.

What Relay never does with it

  • Sell it, or pass it to advertisers, data brokers or anyone who resells information.
  • Use it to show or target adverts.
  • Use it to decide whether to lend to you or to judge your credit.
  • Use it to build, improve or train AI or machine-learning models. Relay does not use information from Gmail or any other Google Workspace API to develop, improve or train generalised or non-personalised AI or machine-learning models, and doesn't let anyone else do so.

Google's rules

Relay's use of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Deleting it

Disconnecting a Gmail inbox deletes every receipt Relay captured from it and the key to it, and cancels Relay's access at Google. Deleting your account does the same for every inbox. You can also remove Relay yourself at any time in your Google Account, under Third-party apps and services (myaccount.google.com/connections). How long each thing is kept is under How long we keep it.

How we use it

  • To show your purchases, return deadlines and what you can still return or swap.
  • To send you a return reminder, but only for items where you ask for one. It goes once, to your sign-in address.
  • To send price alerts and other notifications to your phone, only if you turn them on. A shop can also send an offer on something you're watching; it reaches you as an alert, and the shop never learns who received it.
  • To build your Wrapped from your own receipts.
  • To search by photo, only when you use it on the Discover page. The picture you choose is made smaller on your phone first (which also removes hidden details such as where a photo was taken), then sent to Claude, an AI made by Anthropic, with the list of products shops on Relay sell. Claude says what the product is and which products on Relay are closest. It is told to look only at the product, never to describe or identify people, and to ignore names, messages and other personal details a screenshot may show. Relay doesn't keep the picture or what Claude said.
  • To check how well Relay reads receipts, using counts across everyone (for example, how often a total was read right). When a shop connected to Relay confirms an order of yours, we compare it with what Relay read from that shop's email to you about the same order. Nobody at Relay reads your emails for this, and it changes nothing in your account.
  • To keep your account secure.

We use your data because you've asked us to provide Relay to you, and, for reading your email, because you've given us your permission. We do not sell it, use it for advertising or share it with data brokers.

Your bank and cards (if you connect them)

  • How: through TrueLayer, an open banking company authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority. You sign in on your bank's own page; Relay never sees your bank password.
  • What Relay can do: read your accounts, cards and payments. It can't move money or make payments.
  • What it keeps: each payment out of your account or card from the last 90 days (date, amount, the bank's description, the shop's name, the kind of payment as TrueLayer labels it, such as Shopping or Groceries, and which account), and which receipt it matches. Relay uses the label to spot shop payments that have no receipt yet. Money coming in isn't kept.
  • Why: to match your card payments to your receipts, and to find purchases that never sent a receipt.
  • Stopping: disconnect a bank under Bank and cards (tap your picture) and Relay deletes it and every payment read from it. You can also end the permission at your bank.

Your choices and rights

  • Disconnect your email: Account, then Email connection. This deletes every receipt Relay captured from that inbox. For Gmail, Relay also cancels its access at Google.
  • Withdraw your permission: Account, then Privacy & data. This disconnects your email and deletes what Relay captured.
  • Get a copy of your data: Account, then Download my data. You get one file with everything Relay keeps about you.
  • Delete your account: Account, then Delete my account. This removes your sign-in, email connection, receipts, profile, price alerts, passkeys, two-step sign-in, saved card, and your swap and sell listings and messages. For Gmail, Relay also cancels its access at Google. If you connected Outlook, you can also remove Relay from the apps connected to your Microsoft account.
  • Turn off notifications: on the Price alerts page, or in your phone's settings.

You also have the right to ask for a copy of your data, to have it corrected or deleted, to object to how we use it, and to complain to a data protection regulator. In the UK that is the Information Commissioner's Office, at ico.org.uk.

Keeping it safe

The keys to your inbox and your two-step sign-in are encrypted. Sessions, recovery codes and sign-in links are only stored in scrambled form. Everything travels over encrypted connections.

Children

Relay is meant for adults. Please don't use it if you're under 18.

If we change what Relay collects or how it's used, we'll update this page and tell you in the app. If the change affects what Relay reads from your email, we'll ask for your permission again first.
